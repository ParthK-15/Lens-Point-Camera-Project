Editorial Buying Guide

Through the Lens of Time

Sumati Color Lab · March 2026 · 8 min read
Scroll to read

Photography didn't begin with a smartphone or a sleek mirrorless body — it began with a box, a pinhole, and a whole lot of patience. The story of cameras is ultimately a story about human desire: to capture beauty, document truth, and hold a moment still forever.

01 From Dark Rooms to Digital Dreams

The camera obscura, used as far back as the 11th century, projected images onto surfaces but couldn't capture them. It wasn't until 1826 that Joseph Nicéphore Niépce made the first permanent photograph — an eight-hour exposure of a rooftop in France. From that moment, the world's obsession with freezing time began.

1826

The First Photograph

Niépce captures the world's first permanent image — an eight-hour rooftop exposure that changed everything.

1888

Kodak Democratises Photography

George Eastman launches the Kodak camera with the promise: "You press the button, we do the rest." Photography leaves the darkroom and enters every home.

1960s–70s

The Japanese Revolution

Canon, Nikon, Pentax, and Minolta rise to global dominance. The SLR becomes the tool of photojournalists, artists, and weekend hobbyists alike.

1975

Digital is Born

Kodak engineer Steven Sasson builds the first digital camera — a 3.6 kg contraption that took 23 seconds to capture a black-and-white image. It was laughed off internally. Twenty years later, it was the future.

2000s

The Digital Flood

Digital point-and-shoots flood the market. DSLRs boom. Then the smartphone arrives — and suddenly, everyone is a photographer.

Today

The Mirrorless Era

Lighter, faster, smarter mirrorless bodies retain all the power of interchangeable lenses. Serious photography didn't die — it evolved.

The story of cameras is a story of democratisation — of technology moving from the privileged few to anyone with a curiosity about the world.

02 How to Find Your Perfect Camera

The hardest part of buying a camera isn't the price tag — it's the choice. Before thinking about megapixels or brand names, ask yourself one question: what will I actually shoot?

1

Match the Camera to Your Subject

Portraits & People

A full-frame mirrorless with an 85mm f/1.8 blurs backgrounds beautifully. Flattering, fast, and cinematic.

Travel & Street

Size matters. A compact mirrorless like the Fujifilm X-S20 or Ricoh GR IIIx — great quality, without the back pain.

Wildlife & Sports

You need reach and burst speed. A 150–600mm telephoto zoom paired with a fast-tracking body like the Canon R7.

Video & Vlogging

Look for IBIS, a flip-out screen, and clean HDMI output. The Sony ZV-E1 is purpose-built for this.

2

Understand Sensor Size (Without the Jargon)

Sensor size is one of the biggest factors in image quality. Full-frame delivers the best low-light performance — the choice of professionals. APS-C is smaller and more affordable, producing excellent results for most people. Micro Four Thirds, used by OM System and Panasonic, makes for very compact systems loved for travel and video. 1-inch and smaller sensors live in compacts and smartphones — convenient, but they struggle in dim light.

The takeaway: bigger isn't always better. An APS-C camera in the right hands will outperform a full-frame in the wrong ones.

3

Mirrorless vs. DSLR

DSLR
  • Optical viewfinder — proven & robust
  • Vast legacy lens libraries
  • Great battery life
  • Outstanding second-hand value
VS
Mirrorless
  • Slimmer & lighter bodies
  • Superior autofocus systems
  • Better for video
  • The future of every major brand

For anyone buying new today, mirrorless is the direction every major manufacturer is heading.

4

The Lens Matters More Than the Body

Here's a truth experienced photographers know and beginners often learn the hard way: the lens matters more than the body. Camera bodies get upgraded every few years. A great lens lasts a lifetime.

When starting out, a 35mm or 50mm prime is versatile, optically excellent, and teaches you to see light and composition without the crutch of zooming.

5

Set a Realistic Budget

Budget Range What You Get Best For
Under ₹30,000 Entry-level mirrorless or second-hand DSLR + kit lens Beginners, students
₹30,000 – ₹80,000 Mid-range mirrorless with prime or zoom lens Enthusiasts ★
₹80,000 – ₹2,00,000 Advanced mirrorless, high-resolution bodies, pro lenses Serious hobbyists
Above ₹2,00,000 Full-frame flagships, cinema cameras Working professionals

Remember: accessories add up. Budget separately for a memory card, extra battery, camera bag, filters, and a cleaning kit.

6

Hold It Before You Buy It

Ergonomics are deeply personal. Some photographers love the chunky grip of a Nikon DSLR; others prefer the compact elegance of a Fujifilm X-series. Whenever possible, visit our store and hold the bodies you're considering. A camera you enjoy using is one you'll actually pick up.

The Bottom Line

Photography is one of the few hobbies where the barrier between beginner and master is almost entirely in the eye, not the equipment. Whether you're drawn to the tactile pleasure of film, the immediacy of a compact digital, or the limitless versatility of a full-frame mirrorless, there is a camera out there that was made for the way you see.
